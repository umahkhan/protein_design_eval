Definitions, scope and reproducibility
  • Match to natural sequence (also called native sequence recovery): share of positions where a design uses the same amino acid as the experimentally observed protein. It does not show that a design folds or works.
  • Variety: on average, the share of positions that differ between two designs for the same protein.
  • Temperature controls how adventurous the sampling is. Each tool has its own scale, so the same number does not mean the same behaviour.
  • How numbers are averaged: for one setting (e.g. ProteinMPNN at T 0.1), average the 20 designs of each protein, then average across the proteins, each counting equally.
  • Inputs: only the backbone atoms of one chain from an experimental structure. The natural sequence is used afterwards for scoring only.
  • Scope: {{n_struct:0}} proteins (8 per family), computational only, no lab or folding validation. Family-level results are descriptive. Some proteins may be re-determinations of structures the models saw in training; this was not analysed.
  • Timing: M2 Pro CPU, batch of 1. {{n_retimed:0}} settings slowed by machine load were re-measured and gave identical sequences.
  • Before any lab work: re-folding and stability checks, family-specific goals agreed with scientists, and controls such as the native and shuffled sequences.
